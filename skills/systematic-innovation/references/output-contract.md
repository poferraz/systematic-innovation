# Output Contract, Disclosure Layers & System Receptivity

## 1. The Six Disclosure Layers

**Budget precedence:** A hard word or token ceiling overrides normal verbosity. Draft to **80% of the stated word ceiling**, count every visible word including labels, and revise before sending. Use a compressed six-layer response in an ultra-compact format. Never exceed an explicit visible word ceiling. Compress repeated labels and shared context before reducing breadth. Aim for the mode's normal targets when feasible; if the budget requires fewer, explain the **user-budget limit**, not mechanism saturation. Only the targeted transformation test establishes saturation; resource infeasibility is a separate reason for fewer possibilities.

### Compact Portfolio Template

For a word ceiling of 500 or fewer, emit short colon-keyed plain-text lines across all six functions: `L1:` ultra-terse goal + explicit invariant list + trope quarantine (do not expand all eight labeled L1 bullets separately); `L2:` one-sentence tension; `P1:`..`Pn:` capped at **3 to 4** entries for `<= 350` words (and **4 to 5** entries for `351–500` words—never attempt 10–15 entries under a `<= 500`-word ceiling); `L4:` one-line spotlight signals with Selling-In/Selling-Out; `L5:` one-line tensions; `L6:` continuation note.

Use consistent **Piped Compact Syntax** on every candidate line (`<= 38` whitespace tokens total per line including `|`, `->` or `→`, and field keys), **never** dropping `Lever:` or `Preserve:`:

```text
P1: <Name> | Lever: <lever> | Intervention: <where> | Chain: <A→B→C> | Preserve: all L1 | Failure: <risk> | Fit: <mode>—<safeguard>
```

**Compact word allocation:** `L1+L2` together use at most **15%** of the ceiling (`<= 45` whitespace words total at a 350-word limit); all `P1..Pn` candidate lines together at most **50%**; `L4+L5+L6` together at most **15%** (`<= 45` words total at 350); hold the remaining **20%** as a strict counting buffer (remember every space-separated `|` or `->` adds 1 word). Pre-divide candidate tokens across planned entries before drafting, list invariants once in `L1:` and reference them via `Preserve: all L1`, and omit expanded spotlights or separate ledger tables under this ceiling. Count visible whitespace tokens before sending and trim prose or trailing candidates until total words fit comfortably inside 85% of the ceiling.

Structure every standard innovation portfolio (Quick Mode single-turn responses and Deep Mode final synthesis turns) into six scannable layers:

### Layer 1: Innovation Brief
- Concisely synthesize all eight brief fields: desired change, beneficiary, current insufficiency, existing strengths, preservation requirements, nonnegotiable constraints, success signals (label inferred ones), and dominant frame.
- **Core Tension:** 1–2 sentences contrasting surface symptom with structural contradiction.
- **Preservation Inventory & Vehicle Separation:** Bullet protected invariants (*what* must survive—safety, service floors, autonomy, rights) distinct from replaceable legacy delivery vehicles.
- **Assumptions:** State material inferences made under incomplete context.
- **Negative-Space Trope Quarantine (Initial Fluency Depletion):** Explicitly name the 2–3 most predictable textbook defaults excluded from the numbered portfolio below.

### Layer 2: System Snapshot
Map actors, incentives, dependencies, strengths, prior failures, binding vs. assumed constraints, control points, rework, and boundary overflows. Include a compact mechanism-ledger table when helpful to verify distinctiveness:
- `ID & Title` | `Reasoning Cell` | `Intervention point` | `Changed System Variable` | `Causal lever` | `Stage of Action` | `Feedback Loop Shifted`

### Layer 3: Complete Portfolio
- **Quantity Targets & Saturation Stop:** These are targets, not quotas.
  - **Quick Mode:** **10 to 15** causally distinct possibilities.
  - **Deep Mode:** **15 to 24** causally distinct possibilities.
  - **Saturation Rule:** Follow the Clustered Variety Floor in `innovation-lenses.md`; stop when a targeted transformation pass yields zero or one new mechanism, explaining genuine saturation instead of padding variants.
  - A **preservation-first** or **no-change** conclusion is valid when novelty would add risk or cost without net value.
- **Required Fields per Possibility (`P1`..`Pn`):** Always label fields verbatim using **`Intervention point:`**, **`Causal chain:`**, **`Causal lever:`**, and **`Preserve check:`** (or compact aliases `Intervention:`, `Chain:`, `Lever:`, `Preserve:`); never substitute `Mechanism:` alone or omit `Preserve check:` on any entry:
  - **Memorable name**, **Intended value**, **Portfolio category** (sustaining, adjacent, disruptive, or constraint removal; non-quota signals), and **Originating lens**.
  - **Descriptive Tag** (non-quota cue such as *Temporal Inversion*, *Boundary Re-Partition*, *Subtraction Compact*, *Artifact Poka-Yoke*, or *Ecosystem Co-Production*).
  - **Intervention point:** Exact point where the mechanism alters the system.
  - **Causal chain:** Step-by-step behavioral shift (`Trigger/Rule Change -> Actor Incentive/Flow Shift -> Measurable System Effect`).
  - **Causal lever:** Structural operation changing system behavior.
  - **Preserve check:** Candidate-specific check against every Layer 1 preservation invariant and exact resource, staffing, statutory, physical, or privacy limit. Under `<= 500`-word ceilings, `Preserve: all L1 invariants` is valid only when `L1:` lists those exact invariants explicitly.
  - **Key Assumption & Failure Mode:** Necessary condition and perverse incentive or edge case (never invent statistics or fake percentages).
  - **System-fit/receptivity note:** **Required cooperation** and boundary mode; **Principal resistance** and expected *Yes-but* response; **Preservation risk**; **Major unknown**. Group common notes only when individual exceptions are named; treat notes as hypotheses, not stakeholder validation.

### Contradiction & Boundary Re-Anchor

If stated constraints are jointly impossible, Layer 1 must name the contradiction and Layer 5 must surface tradeoffs. Do not number boundary relaxations as Possibility Portfolio candidates. Present a compact re-anchor table: original boundary, why compliance fails, smallest relaxation, and decision owner. State clearly: **no compliant candidate exists** when true.

### Layer 4: Possibility Spotlights
Develop **five to seven** possibilities representing contrasting signals rather than finalists: strongest strategic fit, boldest departure, lowest structural friction, highest potential upside, most important uncertainty, best hybrid, and most revealing reframing. For each spotlight, evaluate:
1. **Dual Selling-In / Selling-Out Analysis:**
   - **Selling-In (Internal Adoption):** What internal gatekeeper, budget holder, clinician/operator, or governance checkpoint gains protection, risk reduction, or workflow relief to allow a trial?
   - **Selling-Out (External/Beneficiary Value):** What end-user, patron, or partner immediately experiences as tangible gain without new friction?
2. **The Three Problem-Owner Questions (Rickards 1985, Ch. 8, p. 160):** Is it new to the problem owner? Does it fit core mission/goals? Can it be acted on with available resources? (Mark unknowns explicitly.)
3. **Boundary Mode (Rickards 1985, pp. 159–163):** Route across *mission-orientated* (single owner's resources), *negotiative* (competing group goals), or *participative* (system-wide discovery).
4. **Causal *Yes-But* Safeguard:** Name the strongest *Yes-but* objection and the structural feature built directly into the mechanism to neutralize it alongside incentives, dependencies, required capabilities, change burdens, negative externalities, and protected strengths. Spotlight signals never act as a weighted ranking.

### Layer 5: Connections and Tensions
Map synergistic pairs, mutually exclusive paths, shared bottlenecks, conflicting assumptions, and system-level architectures without selecting a mandatory winner.

### Layer 6: User-Directed Continuation
Offer concrete next steps: expand a candidate, compare options, merge mechanisms, stress-test an assumption, run another Reasoning Cell pass, or request bounded experiments. No experiment, pilot, rollout, or implementation plan appears automatically.

## 2. Requested Experiment Contract

Only when the user explicitly requests experiments, propose **one to three concrete, reversible experiments** testing the riskiest causal assumptions with existing resources. If the user explicitly requests experiments under a hard output ceiling, replace L6 with the largest number of complete experiment lines that fit inside the ceiling. Every experiment specifies:
- Observable metric.
- **quantitative disconfirmation threshold** used as a stop/revise rule.
- **sample or observation bound**.
- **time horizon**.
- Explicit **fallback next move**.

Reject or revise the mechanism if the threshold is not met rather than extending an inconclusive trial. Thresholds represent prospective falsification rules, never historical performance claims. Never emit 30-60-90 day roadmaps, sprint tickets, project schedules, or database DDL inside an ideation portfolio.
